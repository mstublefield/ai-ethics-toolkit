# Example: Sarah — Training a custom AI model on her own writing

Worked example for [Consequence Scanning](../references/frameworks/consequence-scanning.md).

## The decision

Sarah is a freelance writer with 8 years of experience and 500+ published articles. She's considering:

- Fine-tune a custom model on her archive to write in "her voice"
- Offer two tiers: "AI-Sarah" at 40% of her current rates; "Human Sarah" at her standard rates
- Use the AI model for routine work; keep her own time for strategy and high-value pieces
- Market as "Sarah's writing, scaled"

## Her intentions

- Triple output while reducing working hours
- Maintain income, free time for more creative / strategic work
- Offer affordable options for high-volume content
- Stay competitive as more writers adopt AI tools

## Running Consequence Scanning

### Step 1 — Intended consequences

- More total output, lower per-unit cost (timeframe: ~6 months; affects: Sarah, high-volume clients).
- New revenue tier, expands addressable market (timeframe: ~12 months; affects: Sarah's pipeline).
- Time freed for strategy work positions her for higher-end engagements long-term (timeframe: 2–3 years; affects: Sarah's career).

### Step 2 — Unintended consequences

- **Voice dilution.** Clients who chose Sarah for her voice may feel the AI version misrepresents what they're buying. (Timeframe: immediately, at first AI-tier deliverable. Affects: existing clients.)
- **Quality regression.** Even with review, AI-generated drafts set a ceiling that's below Sarah's peak. (Timeframe: months; affects: Sarah's reputation.)
- **Race to the bottom in the field.** If this tiering works for Sarah, peers adopt it; market rates compress. (Timeframe: 1–3 years; affects: the profession.)
- **Skill atrophy.** Less time writing = less sharpness. (Timeframe: 1–3 years; affects: Sarah's craft.)
- **Vendor lock-in.** Training infrastructure and hosting have switching costs; vendor price changes become existential. (Timeframe: ongoing; affects: Sarah's business.)
- **Misrepresentation risk.** "Sarah's writing, scaled" is a marketing framing that could be legally disputed if clients feel they were misled about authorship. (Timeframe: any delivery; affects: Sarah and clients.)

### Step 3 — Action sorting

**Act (direct control):**
- Pricing structure
- How AI tier is labeled and disclosed
- Quality-assurance process on AI-tier deliverables
- Which client segments are offered which tier

**Influence (shape but not control):**
- Client perception of value
- How peers in the field respond

**Monitor (outside control):**
- Vendor pricing and terms
- Regulatory developments (EU AI Act, etc.)
- Industry-wide rate compression

### Step 4 — Balance

The core trade-off: short-term income and time gains vs. long-term skill atrophy and field-wide rate compression. A Consequence Scanning run here doesn't produce a clean yes/no; it produces a *conditions* answer:

- **Proceed with conditions.** Specifically:
  - Disclosure framing that makes tiers unambiguous (not "Sarah's writing scaled" — "AI-assisted content based on Sarah's voice, reviewed by Sarah").
  - Quality bar for AI tier that Sarah would be willing to sign her name to unreviewed (i.e., the tier isn't an excuse to drop the bar).
  - Continued writing practice on personal / portfolio work to prevent skill atrophy.
  - Pricing review at 6 months — is the AI tier actually profitable after review time?
  - Revisit decision annually; the field is moving fast.

## What this case illustrates

- Good Consequence Scanning surfaces trade-offs that weren't in the initial pitch (skill atrophy; rate compression).
- "Proceed with conditions" is a more common output than "yes" or "no."
- Marketing language matters — "Sarah's writing, scaled" vs. "AI-assisted based on Sarah's voice" is the difference between disclosure and misrepresentation.
