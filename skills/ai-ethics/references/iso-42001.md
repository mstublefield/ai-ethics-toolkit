# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Quick Reference

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the international management-system standard for AI. It's modeled on ISO 27001 (information security) and ISO 9001 (quality), so the clause structure will feel familiar to anyone who's been through those.

Use `ethics crosswalk --policy file.md --against iso_42001` for a keyword-presence check.

## Clause structure (abridged)

- **Clause 4 — Context of the organization.** Scope, interested parties, internal/external issues.
- **Clause 5 — Leadership.** Top-management commitment; AI policy; roles, responsibilities, authorities.
- **Clause 6 — Planning.** Risk and opportunity identification; AI objectives; risk assessment and treatment plan.
- **Clause 7 — Support.** Resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information.
- **Clause 8 — Operation.** Operational planning and control; AI system impact assessment; lifecycle processes.
- **Clause 9 — Performance evaluation.** Monitoring, measurement, audit, management review.
- **Clause 10 — Improvement.** Nonconformity, corrective action, continual improvement.

## How to apply in small-practice context

ISO 42001 is designed for organizations seeking certification — multi-person teams with document-control systems, formal audits, and a compliance function. For a solo practitioner, the value is:

- **Clause 6 (risk planning)** translates well to any size: identify AI risks, rate them, plan treatment.
- **Clause 8 (operation)** is where "how I use AI throughout my process" from your policy template lives.
- **Clause 9 (performance evaluation)** is where "review this policy quarterly and after challenging projects" comes from.
- **Clause 10 (improvement)** is where policy updates land.

Clauses 4 and 5 (context and leadership) are more meaningful for organizations than for individuals, though the spirit of Clause 5 — "there is a named person responsible" — is always relevant.
