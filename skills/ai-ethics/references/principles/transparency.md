# Transparency

> Undisclosed AI use breaches client trust. The uncomfortable truth is that in many professional contexts, clients are paying for human judgment — hiding AI use misrepresents what they're buying.

## What this principle covers

Transparency is about what you disclose, to whom, and when. It's context-dependent: a copy editor using Grammarly doesn't need per-project disclosure; a strategy consultant running the client's proprietary research through ChatGPT probably does. The gradient isn't mysterious — ask: *would this client feel misled if they found out later?*

## Scopes of disclosure

- **Incidental use** (grammar checkers, image compression, IDE autocomplete) — usually not disclosed per project.
- **Substantive assistance** (structural outlines, analysis, significant drafting help) — disclosed before engagement starts.
- **Generated content passed as yours** (AI-authored prose submitted without meaningful human editing) — this isn't a disclosure question, it's a contract/fraud question.

## The diagnostic question

*Can you explain your AI use honestly to the client, on the phone, right now?* If that makes you feel icky, the disclosure is overdue, or the use case is wrong.

## Practical requirements

- Document which tools were used on which deliverable.
- Keep the policy on the same page as the engagement terms — not in a footer.
- Update the policy in writing when you change tools or scopes.
- When clients ask "did you use AI?" — answer the actual question, not a deflection.

## Anti-patterns

- **"It's just a tool"** deflection — if the tool did 60% of the work, that's not just a tool.
- **Disclosure in tiny print** — GDPR-style "we may use your data" language that no one reads.
- **Retroactive disclosure** — telling the client after delivery that AI was involved. Too late for informed consent.

## Cited precedent

- 2024 US academic incident: students discovered their professor was generating lecture notes with ChatGPT, including fabricated citations. The class had been prohibited from using ChatGPT. Students demanded refunds, citing misrepresentation of what they were paying for.

## Crosswalk

- **NIST AI RMF:** "accountable and transparent" trustworthy characteristic.
- **EU AI Act:** Art. 13 (transparency to deployers), Art. 50 (transparency to natural persons; AI-generated content labeling).
- **ISO 42001:** Clause 7 (support: communication) and Annex A (disclosure controls).
