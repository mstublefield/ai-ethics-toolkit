# Quality

> The AI discourse privileges speed and cost. Quality is where professional reputation lives — and it's the first thing unthinking AI use erodes.

## What this principle covers

Quality in AI-assisted work isn't the model's ceiling; it's the human's floor. AI can accelerate drafting, research, analysis, and review — but the decisions about *what counts as good* remain yours. When those decisions are offloaded, quality regresses to whatever the model's default is, which is rarely what your clients are paying for.

## The iron triangle

Borrowed from product development: a service can be **good, fast, or cheap — pick two.** AI has pushed "fast" and "cheap" at the same time without accounting for the third. If you're pricing and marketing on fast-and-cheap, something is paying for it, and in professional services the something is quality.

## Practical checks

- **Quality benchmark before AI was added.** What's your baseline? You need to be able to answer when quality regresses.
- **Review process documented and followed.** Not "spot-check occasionally." Every AI-touched deliverable gets the same review depth as a fully-human one.
- **Where AI can't help.** Strategic positioning, taste, reading between the lines in a client call. Name these explicitly so you don't accidentally automate them.
- **Regression detection.** Periodic revisit of delivered work — did quality drift?

## Why this is an ethics principle

Most AI ethics frameworks list four principles (transparency, accountability, privacy, fairness). Quality is added here because in small-practice and solo-practitioner contexts, *quality is the reason the client is paying you*. Unexamined AI use erodes the product while preserving the price — that's a trust violation even when no other ethical principle is breached.

## Anti-patterns

- **"It's good enough"** — for whose threshold? Whose ceiling?
- **Post-hoc polishing** — AI draft + light human edits doesn't equal AI-free quality. Measure.
- **Price competition with AI-driven peers** — a race to the bottom is still a race to the bottom.

## Crosswalk

Not in NIST RMF or EU AI Act as a separate characteristic — usually folded under "valid and reliable" or contractual obligations. In small-practice context, it's worth calling out on its own.
