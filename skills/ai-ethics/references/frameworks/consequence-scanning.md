# Consequence Scanning

Use for **major, business-changing** decisions: launching a new service, training a custom model, restructuring pricing, substituting significant human work with AI.

CLI: `ethics worksheet scan --out scan.md`.

Originates in Agile product development practice (see *Consequence Scanning: An Agile Practice for Responsible Innovation*, Doteveryone, CC BY-SA 4.0). This distillation is independent of any specific proprietary curriculum.

## The four steps

### Step 1 — Intended consequences
The positive outcomes you're aiming for. For each: what happens, on what timeframe, who benefits?
- What positive changes to your work, income, or offering are you hoping for?
- How will this improve client relationships?
- How will this position you competitively?
- How will this sustain your business long-term?
- What does success look like in 3–5 years?

### Step 2 — Unintended consequences
Negative outcomes that could happen even though you don't mean them to. This is where honesty costs the most and pays the most.
- What might happen to your skills and professional reputation?
- How might established clients react?
- What are the cumulative effects if many people in your field did the same thing?
- What dependencies or vulnerabilities are you creating?
- Who might be harmed, even unintentionally?
- What might go wrong in 3–5 years?

### Step 3 — Action sorting
Take every consequence from steps 1 and 2 and sort it:
- **Act** — within direct control (pricing, disclosure, quality control).
- **Influence** — shapeable but not controllable (client perception, industry norms).
- **Monitor** — outside control, worth watching (competitor moves, platform policy shifts).

### Step 4 — Balance
Step back and ask:
- Can the unintended risks be mitigated?
- Can the intended rewards be captured?
- Is this a good business move? A good ethical move?

Rarely a clean yes/no — the point is that the trade-off is now visible instead of invisible.

## How to apply

- Work in actual writing. The output matters; the thinking matters more.
- Do this before the decision feels urgent. Once you're committed, the exercise warps toward justifying what you've already chosen.
- Revisit as information changes — new tools, new regulations, new client responses all shift the analysis.
