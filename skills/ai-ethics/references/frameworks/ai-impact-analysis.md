# Five-Layer Impact Analysis

Use for **medium-scale** decisions: adding AI to an existing workflow, changing how a process runs, introducing a new tool into client work.

CLI: `ethics worksheet impact --out imp.md` for a blank worksheet, or `ethics impact-analyze --scaffold --out answers.yaml` to work through structured inputs, then render via `ethics impact-analyze --in ... --out ...`.

## The five layers

Work outward. Each layer has three thought-starters — write actual answers, not headers.

### 1. Personal — me
1. How does this AI use affect my professional reputation and credibility?
2. What liability or responsibility am I taking on?
3. Am I maintaining or developing the skills that define my professional value?

### 2. Relational — the people I work with
1. Is my client receiving the quality and type of work they believe they're purchasing?
2. Am I being transparent with collaborators about my AI use?
3. How might end users be affected by bias, errors, or lack of transparency?

### 3. Business — my company
1. How does this affect my business sustainability and competitive position?
2. What are the financial and reputational risks if something goes wrong?
3. Am I creating dependencies on AI tools that could threaten my business if they change, disappear, or raise prices?

### 4. Industry — my field
1. Am I contributing to raising or lowering professional standards?
2. How does this affect pricing expectations and the perceived value of human expertise?
3. What precedent am I setting for how AI should integrate into professional services?

### 5. Society — the world
1. What are the broader implications if everyone in my position made similar choices?
2. Am I contributing to beneficial or harmful societal trends around AI adoption?
3. What would an ideal future of human-AI collaboration look like, and do my decisions move us closer to it?

## Synthesis

After working the five layers, look across them.

- **Compounding risks** — where does the same concern show up at multiple layers? That's where the stakes multiply.
- **Compounding benefits** — where does the same upside show up at multiple layers? That's where the case is strongest.
- **Recommended posture** — proceed, proceed with conditions, or stop.

There's rarely a clean yes/no. The point is making the trade-off visible.

## Precedent

This structure draws on standard stakeholder-impact-analysis patterns used in product ethics (e.g., Doteveryone's Consequence Scanning, NIST AI RMF's "MAP" function). The specific five-layer ordering here is a simplification for solo practitioners.
